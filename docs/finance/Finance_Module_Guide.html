Finance

What each property costs, what it earns, and whether it pays its way — today, and where it is heading.

The question it answers: “Is this property paying for itself, or is it a loss to us?”

1The idea

Every property either brings money in, costs money to keep, or both. The Finance module records both sides against each property and sets them against each other.

Take the LNU Nugegoda school as an example. We pay rent for the building and its running costs — rates, utilities, maintenance. The school earns fees. If we bought the land, that purchase is a cost too, but a different kind: an investment. The module answers, for any period and for any property:

Does it pay its way?

Income less running costs gives the net result, and a status: profitable, break-even, loss-making or cost only.

How much is rent?

Rent is shown on its own, and as a share of the income — the clearest sign of a property where rent eats the business.

Is it getting better?

Month by month over up to two years, with a trend and a 12-month projection: when a loss-maker should break even.

2What you record: three kinds of money

Every amount is an entry against one property. It is one of three kinds, and the kind decides how it is counted:

↑ Income

Money the property brings in.

  • Business revenue (e.g. school fees, sales)
  • Rent received from a tenant
  • Other income

↓ Running cost

What it takes to keep the property going.

  • Rent / lease
  • Rates & taxes
  • Maintenance & repairs
  • Utilities
  • Insurance
  • Security & cleaning
  • Site staff & admin
  • Other running cost

🏛 Capital

Money invested in the property — kept apart.

  • Land / property purchase
  • Construction & renovation
  • Equipment & fit-out
  • Legal, stamp duty & fees
💡Why capital is kept apart. Buying land is an investment, not a loss in the month it is paid. If it were counted as a running cost, every property would look like a disaster the year it was bought. Capital is instead set against the result as a return and a payback time.

Payments that cover several months

An entry covers one or more whole months from its first month. A year's rent paid in advance is one entry covering 12 months, and every report counts a twelfth of it in each of those months — so each month and each period shows its true cost, however the bill was paid.

Rent LKR 5,400,000 paid in April for 12 months → LKR 450,000 counted in each month, Apr – Mar A report for Apr – Nov counts 8 × 450,000 = LKR 3,600,000 of it.

Amounts are in Sri Lankan rupees (LKR). The financial year runs April to March.

3The Finance page

Open it from the wallet icon in the top bar. It follows the same tenure tabs, search and filters as the Portfolio, so it always describes the same properties — choose “Rented in” and every figure on the page is about rented-in properties only.

Period
Which months the figures cover: this financial year to date (the default), this financial year in full (including months still to come, useful for commitments such as rent paid ahead), last financial year, the last 12 months or this calendar year to date. Your choice is remembered in your browser.
Export
Downloads the report as a CSV file for Excel — one row per property, worst result first (see section 7).
Record entry
Adds income, a running cost or capital against any property you are allowed to record for (section 9).

5How the properties stand

Every property gets one status for the period. The coloured bar shows the mix; click a status to show only those properties in the table.

StatusMeans
↑ ProfitableIncome is more than running costs by over 5%.
= Break-evenIncome and costs within 5% of each other.
↓ Loss-makingRunning costs exceed income by over 5%.
• Cost onlyCosts, but no income recorded — an office, a store, a yard that serves the business rather than earning.
• No figuresNothing recorded for the period yet.

Turning around lists properties losing money today whose trend is improving, with the month they are projected to break even. Biggest losses lists the five worst results, with rent's share of income where that is the cause. Click any of them to open the property.

6Trends over time

A loss today is not the whole story. A property can lose money now and be clearly heading for profit — or be profitable and slipping. Two charts show the pattern.

Net result over time

For all the properties shown: each month's net result as a bar (green above zero, red below), a blue line for the 3-month average that smooths out lumpy months, and the dashed line in the shaded zone — the projection for the next 12 months. The sentence above says whether the result is improving, worsening or steady.

Trends by property

One small chart per property, side by side, most improving first, so patterns stand out at a glance: a loss-maker turning around shows red bars shrinking toward zero and the dashed line climbing; a property slipping shows bars shortening and the line heading down. Each card shows this month's result and the outlook — the break-even month, when it could turn to a loss, or how much it changes a month.

Filter with All, Turning around, Improving, Worsening or Steady. Each chart has its own scale, so compare shapes here and the amounts on the cards. Click a card to open the property.

⚠️A projection is a guide, not a promise. It is a straight line through the recent months, carried forward — it assumes the recent direction continues. With fewer than 4 months of figures it says “too new to tell”; when the figures jump around a lot it warns that the projection is uncertain.

8A property's own panel

Open any property (from the map, a card or a table row) and its panel has a Finance section for the chosen period:

  • its status, income, running costs (with rent) and net result with margin;
  • capital invested and the payback time, when there is capital;
  • the profit trend chart — monthly results, the 3-month average and the projection, with a marker where the line crosses zero — under a plain sentence such as “Improving by about LKR 13,313 a month. At this rate it breaks even around Feb 2028.”
  • its latest entries — click one to correct or remove it (if you may);
  • Record entry, already set to this property, and a link to the Finance page.

9Recording an entry

  1. Property — already chosen when you start from a property's panel.
  2. Income, Running cost or Capital — the kind decides how it is counted.
  3. Category — the list follows the kind; a hint explains each.
  4. Amount (LKR) — commas are fine: 1,200,000.
  5. First month, and the amount covers 1, 3, 6 or 12 months, or another number up to 120. The form confirms how it will be counted — e.g. “LKR 450,000 a month, counted in Nov 2026 – Oct 2027.” Capital is always counted in its own month.
  6. Paid to / received from, reference no., description and notes are optional, but make the record traceable.

Worked examples

Yearly rent paid in advance

Running cost → Rent / lease → LKR 5,400,000 → first month April 2026 → covers 12 months. Counted as LKR 450,000 a month, April 2026 to March 2027.

School fees for a term

Income → Business revenue → the term's fees → the term's first month → covers the term's months. The fees are spread across the term, so a month without collections does not look like a loss.

A one-off repair

Running cost → Maintenance & repairs → LKR 650,000 → August 2026 → 1 month. Counted in August only.

Buying the land

Capital → Land / property purchase → the price; add stamp duty and legal fees as a second capital entry. Shown as capital invested, with the return and payback — never as a running cost.

10How the figures are calculated

FigureCalculation
Net resultincome − running costs
Marginnet result ÷ income × 100Not shown when there is no income.
Rent sharerent ÷ income × 100
StatusBreak-even when |net| ≤ 5% of the larger of income and running costs; otherwise profitable or loss-making by the sign of the net. Cost only when there is no income at all.
Return a year(net result ÷ months in the period × 12) ÷ capital invested × 100
Paybackcapital invested ÷ net result a yearOnly when the yearly result is positive.
TrendThe monthly net results from the first to the last month with figures, over up to the last 24 months (never months not yet lived). A straight line is fitted through them (least squares). Its slope is the change a month.
DirectionImproving or worsening when the slope is more than 0.4% of the property's average monthly turnover (income + costs); steady otherwise. At least 4 months are needed.
ProjectionThe fitted line carried 12 months forward. Break-even is the month a loss-making line crosses zero; could turn to a loss the month a profitable one does — shown when within three years.
UncertainFlagged when there are fewer than 6 months, or the line explains less than 30% of the month-to-month variation (R² < 0.3).

12Test data

To try the module without touching the real register, switch to Test data at the top of the Portfolio. The test set has two years of finance for 24 test properties — profitable, break-even, loss-making and cost-only ones, two that are turning around (Test Leaf School – Matara, Test Bus Yard – Batticaloa) and two that are slipping. The screenshots in this guide come from it. Real data is hidden while you look at test data, and anything you record then is saved as test data.

13Questions

Why does a property show “Cost only”?
It has costs but no income recorded. That is expected for offices, stores and yards that serve the business; for a property that does earn, record its income.
Why does the trend say “Too new to tell”?
It needs at least 4 months with figures. Record past months too (with their own first month) to build the history.
I recorded a year's rent but this month's cost looks small.
That is the spreading at work: a year's rent counts a twelfth in each month it covers.
How do I correct a mistake?
Open the property, click the entry in its Finance section, change it and save — or remove it. Both are recorded in the activity log.
Can I compare only some properties?
Yes — the tenure tabs, search and filters at the top apply to the whole Finance page, its charts and its export.
Does this replace the accounts?
No. It is a management view per property, entered by hand in rupees. The accounting system remains the record of the books.
